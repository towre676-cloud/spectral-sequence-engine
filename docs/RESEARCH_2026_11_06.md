# Reproducible research: a simplicial Hopf-fibration differential and higher Bocksteins

**Status:** Finite, exact computer-assisted calculations; not a formal proof assistant, not a new theorem of topology, and not a general-purpose fibration-to-Serre construction.

This release adds four research interfaces:

- `bicomplex.DoubleComplex`: accepts first-quadrant anticommuting horizontal/vertical boundary maps. Produces both associated first-quadrant spectral sequences of the total complex. Separately computes the E1 and E2 dimensions by the vertical/horizontal homology construction.
- `bicomplex.tensor_double_complex`: produces the Koszul-signed tensor differential from two finite prime-field complexes, then checks both spectral sequences and total homology against field Künneth expectations.
- `graded_couple.GradedExactCouple`: homogeneous exact couples with `deg(i)=deg(j)=0`, `deg(k)=-1`, checked exactness, actual derived couples, and differential matrices by homological degree. This is **singly graded**; no second filtration index or general Adams/Serre geometric exact couple is implemented.
- `bockstein.BocksteinSpectralSequence` and `LiftedBockstein`: compute prime-primary Bockstein pages and higher differential ranks from integral torsion, and independently calculate actual higher differential matrices from integral lifting and quotient representatives. The input is a finite free **integral** chain complex.

## The real simplicial Hopf calculation

The source of the input is a **published triangulated simplicial map** for the Hopf fibration `S¹ -> S³ -> S²` (Madahar–Sarkaria, *Geometriae Dedicata*, 2000), reproduced in the Macaulay2 SpectralSequences documentation:

<https://www.macaulay2.com/doc/Macaulay2-1.25.11/share/doc/Macaulay2/SpectralSequences/html/___Computing_spthe_sp__Serre_sp__Spectral_sp__Sequence_spassociated_spto_spa_sp__Hopf_sp__Fibration.html>

The fixture `research/hopf_fibration.py` transcribes the **36 tetrahedra** of the triangulated three-sphere, closes under taking faces, and maps `a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i` onto the four vertices `a,b,c,d` of the base two-sphere (the boundary of a tetrahedron). Every simplex obtains filtration level `p = (# distinct projected vertices) - 1`, exactly matching preimages of base skeleta. Its output is a 168-simplex filtered complex, with E0 dimensions:

| q \ p | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 12 | 36 | 36 |
| 0 | 12 | 36 | 36 |

The computed E1 dimensions (vertical relative-homology page) are:

| q \ p | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 4 | 6 | 4 |
| 0 | 4 | 6 | 4 |

At E2, exactly the four positions `(0,0), (0,1), (2,0), (2,1)` remain, each of dimension one. The derived `d2` from `(2,0)` to `(0,1)` has **rank one**, leaving E3 with the positions `(0,0)` and `(2,1)` only; hence total homology is one-dimensional in degrees zero and three. This agrees with the **separately published Macaulay2 rational calculation** at E0/E1/E2/E3, not just a manually prescribed differential. We tested fields F2, F3, F5 and F7. A match with rational results does not prove that all primes behave alike; it verifies those four instances.

Reproduce:

```sh
python -m research.hopf_fibration
python -m unittest tests.test_hopf_fibration -v
```

**Boundary of the claim:** This is a direct calculation on a particular published simplicial fibration and the base-skeletal filtration. It does **not** implement arbitrary Serre spectral sequences, twisted coefficient systems, spectral sequences of arbitrary simplicial maps, or fibrations with nontrivial pi1 action on fiber homology. The algebraic tensor-product and staircase examples are not constructions from fibrations by themselves.

## Bockstein algorithm and mathematical checkpoint

Let `C` be a finite free integral chain complex and `p` a prime. From the coefficient exact sequence `0 -> Z --p--> Z -> F_p -> 0` comes a homological Bockstein spectral sequence with

`E1[n] = H_n(C; F_p), beta_r: E_r[n] -> E_r[n-1]`.

If `H_n(C;Z) = Z^b ⊕ T`, then a cyclic p-primary summand `Z/p^s` in `H_n` contributes two dimension-one mod-p entries (at degrees `n` and `n+1`) to E_r for every `1 <= r <= s`, and a rank-one `beta_s` from `n+1` to `n`. The E_infty page has dimensions equal to the free ranks `b` (over F_p). This is the standard higher universal coefficient formula, not a new theorem:

`0 -> (p^(r-1) H_n(C;Z)) ⊗ F_p -> E_r[n] -> Tor(p^(r-1) H_(n-1)(C;Z), F_p) -> 0`.

Background: J. P. May, *A Primer on Spectral Sequences*, Example 2.3:
<https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~may/MISC/SpecSeqPrimer.pdf>.

The `BocksteinSpectralSequence` layer computes these ranks from integer homology, with UCT cross-checks against direct reduction of the integer boundary matrices modulo p. The **distinct** `LiftedBockstein` implementation selects actual E1 homology chain representatives and constructs integral lifts `x` satisfying `d x` divisible by `p^r`. It computes `beta_r[x]` using `d x / p^r` modulo p, and reduces the result by ordinary boundaries and earlier Bockstein images. For an E_r cycle, it corrects the integer lift by a sum of terms `p^r z` (boundary preimages) and `p^(r-i)w_i` (witnesses for previous `beta_i` images), ensuring divisibility by `p^(r+1)`. All operations are exact integer or finite-prime-field arithmetic. When the lift-derived ranks disagree with the independent torsion fingerprint, the program raises an error.

A single two-term Moore complex with boundary multiplication by `p^4` has beta1=beta2=beta3=0 and rank(beta4)=1. Across the regression fixtures, non-diagonal unimodular basis changes and interacting boundaries in several homological degrees require actual corrections—not merely inspection of diagonal matrices.

```python
from integral import IntegralChainComplex
from bockstein import LiftedBockstein
c = IntegralChainComplex([1, 1], {1: [[16]]})
b = LiftedBockstein(c, prime=2)
assert b.page(4) == {0: 1, 1: 1}
assert b.differential(4)[1] == ((1,),)
assert b.page(5) == {}
assert b.verify()
```

Caveats: The specific matrices depend on deterministic basis selection, the torsion invariant factors depend on integer elimination correctness, and computational certificates recompute with this same implementation. The current algorithm is dense and optimized for small finite complexes. It is not a stable persistent Bockstein module for arbitrary filtered spaces; it computes the standard unfiltered integral Bockstein spectral sequence. It does not resolve integral `E_infty` extension ambiguity for an unrelated filtration.

## Graded exact couples and double complexes

A singly graded exact couple consists of exact sequences

`D_n --i--> D_n --j--> E_n --k--> D_(n-1)`.

The derived couple has `D' = im(i)` and `E' = ker(jk)/im(jk)`, with `i',j',k'` descended from actual linear maps, and its differential is `j'k'`. For `D_0=span(a,b)`, `E_0=span(x)`, `E_1=span(y)`, with `i(b)=a`, `j(b)=x`, `k(y)=a`, both the initial first differential and the first page differential vanish; the **second** differential sends `[y]` to `[x]`. The program checks degree and exactness at each derived stage.

For the algebraic double-complex layer, `d_v` lowers `q`, `d_h` lowers `p`, and `d_v d_h+d_h d_v=0`. Filtering the total complex by `p` produces the column spectral sequence. It also computes the row spectral sequence with filtration by `q` and cross-checks their common total homology. E2 is independently computed as homology of the induced cross-axis d1 on E1. Staircase fixtures exhibit first nonzero d2, d3, d4, and d5 over F2, F3 and F5. These are algebraic bicomplexes; they are **not automatically** Serre data.

## Validation and open scientific questions

The regression suite checks graded exactness, arbitrary-prime boundary signs, independent E1/E2 double-complex construction, four Hopf-field instances, Bockstein torsion rank identities, integer lift divisibility, chain-map signs, deterministic replay and tamper detection, plus 100 random multi-degree integral complexes with exact integer basis changes and 180 random two-term non-diagonal presentations. All cases are bounded and finite. No new general topological theorem is claimed.

**Research priorities:** (1) General simplicial-map adapters with verified geometric filtration; (2) twisted local coefficient systems and explicit Serre construction; (3) truly bigraded exact couples and Adams resolutions; (4) integral filtered spectral pages, their extension problems and torsion-aware exact quotient algorithms; (5) sparse elimination and stronger independent proof checking; (6) chain-level mapping cones and Mayer–Vietoris comparison.
